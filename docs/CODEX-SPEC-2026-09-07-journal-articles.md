```mdx
Parrit.ai is a French company that designs and builds company operating systems: the AI system a company operates on, made to order for one client and handed over as that client's property. The company was founded by Paul Larmaraud, who holds the thirty-minute examination that opens every commission himself, on a video call. For three years we have built these systems for large accounts, SMEs and mid-sized companies, one company at a time. Commissioned, not subscribed. Everything we publish lives at one address, [parrit.ai](/).

We call ourselves a manufacture. In software the word usually decorates a landing page. At ours it has a price.

## A manufacture starts from an order

Everything we build exists because one company asked for it. The first act of any commission is [an examination](/commission): thirty minutes with the founder, followed by a written diagnostic of where your flows break, and which operation is worth rebuilding first. You leave with a verdict, a written scope or a clear no. Terms come afterwards, in black and white. A workshop that quotes before it has examined is selling its calendar, whatever it says about your problem.

## The work is done one piece at a time

Construction takes one critical operation and rebuilds it end-to-end, in your accounts, under your keys. It runs in production, with real users, usually within a few weeks. Then comes the rest, piece by piece, and each new capability raises the value of every previous one: the capability that detects a commercial signal hands the case to the one that follows up on it. We call that third phase Compounding, and the whole method, from Examination through Construction to Compounding, is written out on [the Manufacture](/manufacture).

## A manufacture signs what it delivers

Every system we ship is certified to [the Standard](/standard): six commitments, identical for any commission. The state of the system stays readable at any moment. Every signal carries its decision, and every decision keeps its origin, so the journal is the audit. The way back is written in advance. The system belongs to you, and each new brick raises the value of the ones before. The sign-off comes from your team, at delivery. STD-1.0 is our own bar, and we ask clients to hold us to it.

## The client keeps the object

When a manufacture delivers, the object leaves the workshop. Ours do too: code, data and documentation included, running in the client's own accounts. Every system we have delivered is owned this way, by the client, in full. Your team runs it without us. That is why we call what we build owned infrastructure: the asset sits on your side of the ledger, whoever you work with next.

## It runs at ours first

What runs at a client's runs at ours first. Parrit.ai operates on the same kind of system it sells: more than 200 signals become decisions every week on our own infrastructure, received and arbitrated by the founder, figures current as of September 2026. The deliveries hold up after we leave. At a consumer brand, [one dossier](/dossiers) records 2.5 months recovered on a single reporting process. Today that reporting assembles itself and ships on schedule, and no one at Parrit.ai touches it anymore.

There is a quiet test at the end of every commission: our access can end and the system keeps running. The word manufacture was chosen with that moment in mind.
```

## Fichier 2 · `content/journal/what-is-a-company-operating-system.mdx`

```mdx
---
title: "What is a company operating system?"
date: "2026-09-07"
description: "A company operating system reads what happens across a business, frames the decisions that need a human, and executes the actions in the company's own tools. It is built for one company and belongs to it, code and data included."
slug: "what-is-a-company-operating-system"
---

A company operating system is the software layer a company runs its daily operations on: it reads what happens across the business, surfaces the decisions that need a human, and executes the resulting actions inside the tools where the work already lives. It is built for one company, runs in that company's accounts, and belongs to it, code and data included.

That is the definition [Parrit.ai](/) works from, after three years of building these systems for large accounts, SMEs and mid-sized companies, one company at a time. We state it this precisely because the term itself has gone soft.

## A term everyone uses and nobody has settled

Type the phrase, or its cousin "AI operating system", into a search engine and the results disagree with each other. Some describe agent orchestration infrastructure for global enterprises. Others mean a chat interface with seven modules and a per-seat monthly price. For a third group the phrase covers management frameworks, software optional. The meaning shifts with whoever is selling. A definition worth citing has to survive contact with production, so everything below comes from systems that run.

## What a company operating system is not

Three things wear the label and fail the definition.

A rented platform, first. Most of what sells under this term is SaaS: subscription software where you configure your workflows inside the vendor's product, and the vendor keeps the product. The day the subscription lapses, the encoded knowledge of how your company runs lapses with it. An operating system that lives in someone else's product is a tenancy.

A chat interface, second. An assistant answers when someone types. An operating system keeps working between the questions: it watches the pipeline, sends the follow-ups on schedule, and when an invoice blocks, it says so before anyone thinks to ask. Bolting a chat window onto a company speeds up the answers; the pipeline, the invoices and the follow-ups keep moving exactly as they did.

A consultancy that leaves after the audit, third. The diagnostic arrives as a deck, the recommendations are sound, and six months later the company operates exactly as it did before, because nobody built anything into production. The engagement ended where an operating system would have started.

## Three moves

The first move is understanding: everything that happens across the business, readable at any moment. Pipeline state, follow-ups sent, yesterday's incident, on one screen. You open it, you know.

The second is deciding. The system prepares each decision and carries it to a human: a blocked invoice surfaces framed and quantified, on a card, with its context attached. Only decisions reach the operator's desk.

The third move, acting, is where the loop closes. The action executes in the same system, inside the company's actual tools, journaled and reversible: a follow-up sent by mistake takes one click to undo, and the click restores the state as it was before.

## What one rebuilt operation changes

At a consumer brand, a single reporting process was rebuilt. The reporting now assembles itself and ships on schedule, the client's own team runs it today, and the measured result is 2.5 months recovered, on that one process, in the client's own system. In a system built this way each capability joins the same fabric, and the reporting built first can feed the follow-ups built after it.

## The ground nobody holds

In early September 2026 we ran a dozen searches across the English and French markets before writing this piece. Our reading of them, and it is ours: the platforms raising large rounds under enterprise AI operating system banners build for the world's biggest accounts, the subscription products bill by the seat, and the consulting offers stop at the recommendation. Across every search we ran, no offer put three things together at once: France, SMEs and mid-sized companies as the stated client, and a system built for that client then handed over to it.

That combination is the ground Parrit.ai stands on. Each system is commissioned, built in the client's accounts, and delivered with code and data included, owned by the client in full. The method, from Examination through Construction to Compounding, is written out on [the Manufacture](/manufacture).

The definition also gets tested daily where it should be, on our own operations. The first dossier in [our registry](/dossiers) is our own system, filed under the title "We sell the system we run on". More than 200 signals become decisions every week there, received and arbitrated by the founder. What runs at a client's runs at ours first. When someone asks what a company operating system is, that screen is the answer we open.
```
