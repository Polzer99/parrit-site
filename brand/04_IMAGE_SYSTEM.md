# 04 — Système photographique et visuel

## Principle

Parrit imagery must look like a field journal about people building systems, not an advertisement for artificial intelligence.

The image carries the human truth. The design layer carries the system.

Generate or photograph the clean human scene first. Add the red signal, halftone, typography, grids and red-thread diagrams in Figma or code. This keeps the identity stable and prevents every generated image from inventing a different brand.

## Four image families

### 1. Founder — approachable operator

Purpose: trust, contact, editorial covers, conversion.

Paul should look:

- recognisably like the supplied references;
- friendly without a sales grin;
- alert, calm and slightly amused;
- credible as a founder who works in the system;
- naturally textured, not retouched into a model.

Preferred styling:

- black or charcoal blazer;
- white or ecru T-shirt;
- occasional navy pinstripe suit when the context is more formal;
- warm neutral light;
- simple office, studio, café or field environment;
- eye contact or a visible working gesture;
- relaxed shoulders and hands.

Friendly expression rule:

- micro-smile;
- smile visible in the eyes;
- closed mouth or slightly visible teeth;
- no exaggerated grin;
- no severe “tech founder” stare.

### 2. Field documentary

Purpose: prove proximity to teams and real operations.

Scenes:

- workshop with a client team;
- observation of a manual workflow;
- industrial, service or support environment;
- reviewing documents, dashboards or a queue;
- a handoff between operator and agent.

Use real environments and imperfect details. Avoid empty luxury offices.

### 3. Editorial system collage

Purpose: explain complexity and causality.

Ingredients:

- monochrome people or documents;
- off-white paper;
- red halftone;
- thin red arrows and nodes;
- browser windows, cards or sheets only when they represent real steps;
- captions in mono typography.

The collage must answer a question. It is not moodboard decoration.

### 4. Red-thread operating map

Purpose: show an agent’s journey from trigger to output.

- white or off-white environment;
- black objects and people;
- one continuous red line;
- selective motion blur to show flow;
- explicit beginning, gates and end state.

## Crop library

Every approved portrait should be exported in:

- 16:9 with 45–55% negative space for a website hero;
- 4:5 for LinkedIn;
- 1:1 for covers and profile assets;
- 9:16 for video thumbnails and stories;
- transparent or clean-background cutout where possible.

## Treatment

- Contrast: editorial, not crushed.
- Grain: fine, tactile, consistent.
- Saturation: low to neutral.
- Red appears as a graphic layer, not a skin cast.
- Halftone dot size must remain legible at the final output size.
- Do not apply halftone across eyes or the entire face unless making a deliberate poster.

## Negative prompt / rejection list

Reject any image with:

- a different person’s facial structure;
- dark curly hair when Paul’s reference is light brown/blond;
- plastic skin;
- perfect fashion-model jawline replacing identity;
- blue neon or cyberpunk backgrounds;
- floating holographic interfaces;
- robots;
- meaningless code walls;
- exaggerated CEO power pose;
- crossed arms by default;
- fake corporate laughter;
- hands with visible artifacts;
- illegible generated text;
- excessive depth-of-field blur that removes context.

## Master portrait prompt

Create a high-resolution editorial portrait using the supplied photos of Paul Larmaraud as the identity reference. Preserve his real facial proportions, light brown/blond swept hair, natural beard, eye shape, skin texture and age. He is wearing a charcoal or black blazer over an ecru T-shirt. He looks directly at the camera with a subtle warm micro-smile and a smile visible in the eyes; approachable, intelligent and calm, never severe or fashion-model perfect. Natural relaxed posture, shoulders open, hands believable. Contemporary European studio or operational workspace in warm neutral daylight, minimal background, generous negative space on one side for typography. Documentary/editorial photography, tactile fine grain, restrained contrast, natural skin, 50 mm lens feeling. No text, no red graphic overlay, no futuristic interfaces, no neon, no robot, no glossy advertising retouching. The clean photograph will later receive Parrit’s off-white, black, red halftone and red-thread graphic system in Figma.

## Master editorial composition prompt

Create an editorial brand image for Parrit.ai on an off-white paper field. Use black-and-white documentary photography, a single Parrit red signal, controlled halftone texture, thin red arrows or a continuous red thread, mono captions and strong negative space. Show a concrete business workflow moving from manual input to controlled agent output. Every card, document and line must have an explanatory role. The result should feel like a contemporary field journal and an operational design system, not a futuristic AI advertisement. No blue-purple gradient, no hologram, no robot, no 3D blobs, no glassmorphism, no random dashboard.
