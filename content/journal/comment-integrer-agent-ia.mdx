Integrating an agent is not a matter of connecting an API and hoping for the best. It means selecting one business process, taking it apart and rebuilding it with a new automation capability. The objective is to increase a team's capacity on a specific measurable task.

## Which process should be integrated first?

Start with a high-volume, repetitive and low-complexity task. Examples include qualifying inbound insurance quote requests, assigning first-line support tickets or consolidating daily sales reports from a franchise network. Isolate one simple bottleneck and remove it.

## Should a model be trained on proprietary data immediately?

No. Begin with a general model to validate the workflow and team adoption. Proprietary training can come later, after the system has proved useful. A law firm can test public case-law summarization before allowing access to confidential contracts.

## Who should lead the integration project?

The owner of the affected operation, supported by IT. If the agent assists with tenders, the sales director should lead. The person whose problem is being solved must own the system; operational ownership is essential.

## How should success be measured?

Define one or two indicators before writing code. Avoid vague productivity claims. Measure order processing time, first-response time, errors prevented or additional processing capacity. The metric must describe the business effect.

## What is the main risk and how is it avoided?

The primary risk is building a tool nobody uses. Involve end users from the first day, design with them, deploy a simple first version, collect field feedback and iterate. Adoption matters more than technical perfection.
