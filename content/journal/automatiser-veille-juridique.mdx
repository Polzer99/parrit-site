Legal monitoring means tracking the laws, decrees, case law and doctrine that affect you. An automated system watches those sources and alerts you when something changes. You can focus on interpretation and strategy instead of raw monitoring.

## Which sources can an agent monitor?

Anything published online in a structured form: official journals, court websites, recognized specialist publications and paid legal databases with APIs. The agent watches RSS feeds, changing web pages and newsletters forwarded to a dedicated inbox. The first task is an exhaustive list of critical sources.

## How do you specify what it should find?

Use precise queries and filters, not a broad instruction such as “monitor company law.” Provide keywords, phrases, Boolean combinations, article references and standards. Exclusions reduce noise. The agent applies those filters to each new publication it detects.

## What happens when it finds a match?

It extracts the information into a defined template rather than sending a bare link. It can write a two-sentence summary, cite the relevant provision, record the publication date and authority, then deliver the prepared note by email, Slack or a case-management ticket.

## How do you prevent useless alerts and false positives?

Refine the rules week by week. Record false positives, adjust keywords and require context combinations. For example, “contract” might trigger an alert only when “termination” appears in the same sentence. After an initial calibration period, precision improves.

## Should everything be automated?

Monitor the five to ten vital sources continuously, while reviewing secondary sources in a weekly or monthly digest. A short manual review can capture weak signals that strict rules miss. Automation increases monitoring capacity; it does not need to replace judgment.

## What is the first concrete step?

Take the latest legal development that affected you, identify its exact source and note the terms that would have detected it. Monitor that single source with that first rule for one week. If it works, add a second source. Expand from a functional core instead of building a monolith.
